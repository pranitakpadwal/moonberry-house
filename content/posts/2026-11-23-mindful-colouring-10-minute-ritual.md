"Mindful colouring" can sound like one more thing to get right. It isn't. It's just colouring with a little attention, a few minutes of doing one gentle thing and noticing it. Here's a simple ten-minute ritual you can try today, with whatever you already have.

## What you need

- A page with a simple design: a mandala, a flower, big shapes
- Three to five colours you like
- Ten minutes, and a spot where you won't be interrupted

That's it. No special pens, no perfect lighting, no art skill.

## The ten minutes

**Minutes 0–1: Arrive.** Sit down. Put your phone out of reach, or face down and silent. Take three slow breaths, in through the nose, out through the mouth. Let your shoulders drop.

**Minute 1–2: Choose.** Look at your colours and pick the one that feels right *today*. Not the "best" one, just the one you want. Notice that you're choosing.

**Minutes 2–8: Colour one small area slowly.** Pick a single petal or shape. Colour it with unhurried strokes. Feel the pencil on the paper, hear the soft sound it makes, watch the colour fill the space. When your mind wanders (it will), notice it and gently bring your attention back to the next stroke. Wandering isn't failing; coming back is the practice.

**Minutes 8–9: Pause and look.** Put the pencil down and look at what you've coloured, even if it's just a few shapes. There's no need to judge it. Just look.

**Minute 10: Close.** Take one more slow breath. Decide whether to stop here or carry on. Stopping while it still feels good makes tomorrow's session more inviting.

## Tips for making it stick

- **Same time, same place.** Linking it to something you already do, like after dinner or with your morning tea, makes it automatic.
- **Leave the page out.** A half-finished page on the table is an invitation.
- **Keep it small.** Ten minutes you actually do beats an hour you keep postponing.
- **No scorekeeping.** If you miss a day, simply sit down the next. There's no streak to break.

:::tips If it feels hard at first
- Restless? Try colouring faster for a minute, then slow down on purpose.
- Bored? Choose a more detailed page, or try a new colour combination.
- Judging your work? Colour one section with your non-dominant hand. It's hard to be precious about that.
:::

Mindful colouring is a small habit, but small habits are the ones that last. Ten quiet minutes can become a place you return to whenever the day feels too loud.
