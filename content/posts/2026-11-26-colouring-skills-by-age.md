Watching a child's colouring change from big scribbles to careful pictures is one of the quiet joys of early childhood. It is also a handy window into how their hands and attention are growing. Here is a rough map, with the reminder that every child sets their own pace.

## When do kids start colouring inside the lines?

Most children begin to colour mostly inside the lines between four and five, and the skill tends to settle around five to six. Before that, staying inside a printed outline is simply harder than it looks, because it depends on fine control of the hand, steady attention and the ability to plan a stroke.

## An age-by-age guide

**12 to 18 months: exploring marks.** Children grip the crayon in a fist and make big whole-arm scribbles. The point is cause and effect: "I move, and a mark appears." Use chunky crayons and large paper, and stay close.

**2 years: scribbles with intent.** Scribbles become more controlled, with back-and-forth lines and early circles. Children may name their scribble after the fact ("That's a dog").

**3 years: shapes and lines.** Many children can copy a vertical line and a circle, and start holding the crayon with their fingers rather than the whole fist. Colouring is still mostly outside the lines, and that is expected.

**4 to 5 years: more control.** Children start filling shapes and staying mostly inside the edges. Pictures contain recognisable figures such as people, houses and animals.

**5 to 6 years: tidier colouring.** Control tightens. Children can follow outlines more accurately and may start choosing realistic colours.

**7 to 8 years: detail and shading.** Many children enjoy detailed pages, patterns and trying shading or blending with pencils.

## How to support each stage

- Match the page to the stage. Big, simple shapes for younger children, finer detail as control grows.
- Offer variety: chunky crayons, thin pencils, markers and paint all build different skills.
- Try colouring on a vertical surface, like paper taped to a wall, to build shoulder and wrist strength.
- Praise effort and colour choices, not neatness.

:::tips Quick ways to build control
- Playdough squeezing and rolling
- Tearing and sticking paper
- Threading large beads
- Picking up small objects with tongs
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### At what age should a child colour inside the lines?
Most children colour mostly inside the lines between four and five, with the skill settling around five to six. There is a wide normal range, so younger children going outside the lines is expected.

### What colouring skills should a 3 year old have?
Many three-year-olds can copy a vertical line and a circle, hold a crayon with their fingers and make controlled marks. Filling shapes neatly usually comes later, so scribbly colouring is perfectly normal at three.

### How can I help my child colour more neatly?
Offer chunky crayons, bigger and simpler shapes, and activities that strengthen the hands, such as playdough. Avoid correcting their colouring, as pressure can put children off.

### When should I talk to a professional about my child's colouring?
If a child finds holding a pencil very hard or painful, avoids drawing entirely, or seems well behind other children their age in hand skills, mention it to your paediatrician or school. An occupational therapist can look at the full picture.
