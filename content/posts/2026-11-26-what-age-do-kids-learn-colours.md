Few parents can resist asking a two-year-old, "What colour is this?" and then worrying when the answer is "dog." The truth is that learning colours is not one skill but three, and each arrives on its own schedule.

## What age do children learn colours?

Most children begin matching colours around two, name one or two colours by about three, and name the basic colours with growing accuracy between three and five. These are typical ranges, not deadlines. Some children name colours early and others take until well after their fourth birthday, and both can be perfectly healthy.

## Seeing, matching and naming are different skills

**Seeing.** Babies start with a preference for strong contrast, and their colour vision develops over the first months of life. By the time they are toddlers, most can tell many colours apart.

**Matching.** A child can put the red block with the other red blocks long before they can say "red". Matching and sorting usually come first.

**Naming.** Attaching the right word to a colour is a language skill as much as a visual one. Colour words are abstract, because "red" is not an object, so they are learned later than words like "ball" or "cup".

## Why a toddler might call everything "blue"

Children often learn one or two colour words and use them for everything, then sort them out over time. A child who says "blue" for green is usually still working out where one colour ends and the next begins. It is a normal stage and not a sign that anything is wrong.

## When is it worth checking?

If a child is well past four or five and still seems consistently unable to tell certain colours apart, such as red and green, it is reasonable to ask for an eye test. Colour vision differences are fairly common, are usually lifelong, and are easy to support once you know about them. A short test with an eye doctor can give a clear answer.

:::tips Helping without drilling
- Start with two or three colours, not the whole rainbow.
- Sort and match before asking for names: "Can you find another red one?"
- Name colours as you go: "You are holding the yellow cup."
- Let them choose colours when they draw. Choosing is learning.
- Skip flashcard quizzing. It tends to turn a fun game into a test.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### At what age do kids know their colours?
Many children can name some colours by three and most basic colours by four or five. Matching and sorting usually appear earlier, around two. The range is wide, so a later start is often still typical.

### Why does my toddler get colours wrong?
Colour words are abstract and take time to sort out. A child may use one word for several colours for a while. Keep naming colours in daily life and the labels usually settle with practice.

### Can I teach colours to a baby?
Babies are not ready to learn colour names, but they enjoy looking at bold, high-contrast pictures and colourful objects. Talking about what they see builds the language they will use later.

### When should I worry about colour recognition?
If a child past four or five consistently confuses particular colours, an eye test can check for colour vision differences. These are common, usually lifelong and easy to work with once identified.
