A one-year-old with a crayon is not making a picture. They are discovering that moving their hand can leave a mark, and that is a big idea. Here is how to make early colouring safe, simple and fun.

## What does colouring look like at 18 months?

At this age colouring is sensory exploration and hand practice, not drawing. Expect big whole-arm scribbles, a fist grip on the crayon, a lot of tasting and dropping, and sessions that last only a few minutes. All of that is normal and is exactly how the skill grows.

## Are there colouring activities suitable for 1-year-olds?

Yes, and they work best when they are simple:

- **Big paper, big marks.** Tape a large sheet to the floor or a low table so it does not slide.
- **Chunky crayons.** Thick, short crayons are easier for small fists to grip.
- **Finger painting with edible-safe options.** Mushy yoghurt or cooked pasta paint suits children who put everything in their mouths, though you should still supervise closely.
- **Crayon on foil or cardboard.** Different surfaces give different sounds and feels.

## Choosing safe supplies

- Look for crayons and paints labelled non-toxic and suitable for the child's age.
- Choose chunky sizes that are too big to be a choking risk, and throw away broken short stubs.
- Washable products make life easier, but check the label for what "washable" covers.
- Stay with your child the whole time. Even non-toxic products are not food.

## Keep it short and happy

Sessions of five minutes are plenty. Stop when your child loses interest rather than trying to finish a page. Celebrate the marks, not the result: "You made a big circle! Whoosh!"

:::tips A calm setup in two minutes
- Tape paper down
- Offer two or three chunky crayons, not a full box
- Sit beside them and scribble too
- Put everything away together afterwards
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### When can toddlers start colouring?
Many children are ready to make marks with a chunky crayon between 12 and 18 months, with supervision. They are exploring rather than drawing, which is a normal first stage.

### Are crayons safe for toddlers?
Crayons labelled non-toxic and age-appropriate are generally designed for children, but they are not food. Stay with your child, choose chunky sizes and discard broken stubs.

### What are the best colouring supplies for a 2 year old?
Chunky crayons, large paper taped down and washable markers or paint are good starting points. Keep the choices small so they are not overwhelmed.

### How long should a toddler colour for?
Around five minutes is usually plenty. Follow your child's interest and stop when they move on.
