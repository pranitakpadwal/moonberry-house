Choosing paint for a child's room can feel like a big decision, and the internet is full of confident rules. Here is a calmer, more honest approach.

## Which colour is best for a kid's room?

There is no single best colour. Studies on how room colour affects children are limited, so the advice below is practical rather than scientific law. A good rule is to use softer, calmer tones where your child sleeps, and brighter colours where they play or create, while keeping their own taste in mind.

## Think in zones

**Sleep area.** Soft, muted colours such as pale blue, soft green, warm cream or gentle lavender are popular for bedtime spaces, because they feel quiet. Very bright, saturated colours can feel stimulating at bedtime for some children.

**Play area.** This is the place for brighter accents: a bold rug, colourful shelves or a play mat.

**Study or creative corner.** Keep walls fairly neutral and add colour through supplies and artwork, so the room stays easy on the eyes.

## Should a kid's room be colourful?

It can be. Colour makes a room feel cheerful, and many children love it. A simple approach is to keep the big surfaces calm and bring colour in through things you can change easily: bedding, cushions, wall art and toys. That way the room can grow with your child.

## Practical things that matter as much as colour

- **Natural light.** A well-lit room feels brighter whatever the paint.
- **Washable finish.** A wipeable paint saves a lot of stress.
- **Clutter.** A tidy, organised room is calmer than any colour on its own.
- **Your child's voice.** Let them choose a colour or an accent. Rooms feel more theirs when they have a say.

## A note on Vastu

## Frequently asked questions

### What colour is best for a child's bedroom?
Soft, calm colours such as pale blue, green, cream or lavender are popular for sleeping areas. There is no single right answer, so let your child's preferences guide you.

### Should a kid's room be bright and colourful?
It can be, especially in play areas. A good balance is calm walls with bright accents in things you can change easily, such as bedding and rugs.

### Do colours affect how children sleep?
Research is limited. Many parents find softer colours feel more restful, and very bright colours can feel stimulating. Light, noise and routine usually matter more.

### How do I let my child help choose the colour?
Offer two or three calm options for the walls and let them pick accents like bedding or posters. It gives them ownership without overwhelming the room.
