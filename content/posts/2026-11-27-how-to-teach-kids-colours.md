Teaching colours does not need flashcards or a special curriculum. Children soak up colour words most easily when they are woven into play and everyday life. Here is how to do it, with eight activities to try.

## How do you teach a child colours?

Begin with just two or three contrasting colours, such as red and blue, rather than a full rainbow. Let your child match and sort objects by colour before you ask them to name anything. Then name colours naturally as you talk about what you are doing. Naming comes after recognising, so keep it light and unhurried.

## Eight play-based activities

1. **Colour of the day.** Pick one colour each morning and point it out through the day: red cup, red shoes, red bus.
2. **Sorting trays.** Give a child a muffin tin and a bowl of mixed coloured objects, such as blocks or pompoms, and have them sort by colour.
3. **Colour hunt.** Ask them to find three things in the room that are yellow, or take a walk looking for green.
4. **Rainbow collage.** Tear coloured paper and stick it in colour groups.
5. **Colour mixing.** Squeeze two paint colours into a zip bag and squish them together to see what happens.
6. **Colour by number.** Colour-coded pictures give each colour a clear job. Our free [colour game](/play/) works this way.
7. **Colourful snack plate.** Name the colours of the fruit and vegetables on the plate.
8. **Matching games.** Use paint swatches or coloured cards and find the pairs.

## Keep it low pressure

Children learn best when they do not feel tested. If a child gets a colour wrong, simply say it back kindly: "That is a green leaf." Repeating the correct word without making a fuss is more effective than correcting.

:::tips Simple habits that help
- Describe colours as you play and talk.
- Offer a few colours at a time.
- Let your child choose the colours they draw with.
- Repeat favourite games. Children enjoy repetition.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best way to teach toddlers colours?
Use play. Start with two or three colours, let them match and sort, and name colours during everyday activities. Games and hands-on activities work better than flashcards.

### How do I introduce colours to a child?
Pick one colour at a time, point it out in daily life, and use objects they can hold and sort. Add a new colour once they are comfortable with the first ones.

### What colours should I teach first?
Many parents start with bold primary colours like red, blue and yellow, because they are easy to tell apart. There is no strict order, so following your child's interests works well.

### What if my child is not learning colours?
Every child learns at their own pace. If a child past four or five consistently confuses particular colours, ask about an eye test, since colour vision differences are common and easy to support.
