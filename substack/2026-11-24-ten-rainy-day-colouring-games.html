Ten Rainy-Day Colouring Games That Need Nothing but Paper

Ten quick, low-prep games using only paper and crayons, to turn a grey afternoon into a happy, screen-free one.

Quick answer: Ten paper-and-crayon games for a rainy day need no prep: colour dice doodle, rainbow hunt, mystery mixing, one-colour challenge, pass the page, blind drawing, feelings rainbow, colour by number your way, pattern train and gratitude postcard.

Rainy afternoons have a way of stretching. Here are ten colouring games that need nothing but paper and a few crayons, with no prep, no shopping and no mess to speak of.

  1. Colour Dice Doodle. Pick six colours and number them 1 to 6. Roll a die (or just choose a number), draw a shape in the matching colour, and keep going until the page is full.
  2. Rainbow Hunt. Race around the room finding one object for each colour of the rainbow, then draw each one in the right crayon.
  3. Mystery Mixing. Draw a big shape and colour half in one colour, half in another, overlapping a little in the middle. What new colour appears?
  4. One-Colour Challenge. Everyone picks a single colour and draws a whole scene using only that colour and different shades of pressure, light to dark.
  5. Pass the Page. Each person draws for one minute, then passes the page to the next person, who adds to it. Nobody is allowed to say "that's wrong".
  6. Blind Drawing. Close your eyes and draw a face, a house or an animal. Open them and add colour to make sense of it. The funnier, the better.
  7. Feelings Rainbow. Colour a stripe for each feeling you've had today and talk about each one.
  8. Colour by Number, Your Way. Draw a simple picture, divide it into shapes, write a number in each and make a colour key. Swap with someone to solve.
  9. Pattern Train. Draw a long strip of repeating patterns (red-blue-red-blue, or red-red-blue) and challenge a sibling to continue it.
  10. Gratitude Postcard. Draw a small picture for someone you love and write one line on the back. You can post it, or deliver it by hand.

Keeping it going

  • Put one game on a slip of paper in a jar and let your child pick.
  • Keep the tools within reach and visible. Easy access means easier starting.
  • Join in for the first five minutes. Often that's all the push they need.

Frequently asked questions

What can kids do on a rainy day with just paper and crayons?

Plenty: games like colour dice doodle, rainbow hunt, pass the page and pattern train need nothing more than paper and a few crayons.

How do I get my child started without a screen?

Keep the supplies visible and within reach, put one game on a slip of paper in a jar for your child to pick, and join in for the first five minutes.

Which of these games suit different ages?

Mystery mixing and rainbow hunt suit younger children, while colour by number your way and pattern train suit older ones. Pass the page works for the whole family.
